Consciousness as a Field-Mediated Phenomenon:
A White Paper on the Informational Field Hypothesis
Abstract
This white paper presents a comprehensive framework proposing that consciousness is not generated by the brain but is fundamentally anchored to biological systems via a universal informational field, with DNA functioning as a fractal antenna/receiver. We synthesize evidence from quantum biology, biophoton research, near-death experience studies, and theoretical physics to challenge the materialist paradigm of consciousness. The paper argues that the electromagnetic and quantum properties of DNA enable it to couple with a universal informational field—conceptualized as a "cosmic wireless access point"—allowing consciousness to be received, filtered, and rendered by neural networks rather than produced by them. We present testable hypotheses, address competing explanations, and propose experimental protocols to validate this framework.

1. Introduction
1.1 The Hard Problem Revisited
For over three centuries, neuroscience has operated under the assumption that consciousness is an emergent property of neural activity. However, the "Hard Problem of Consciousness"—explaining why and how physical processes in the brain give rise to subjective experience—remains unresolved (Chalmers, 1995). Despite decades of neuroimaging and computational modeling, the explanatory gap persists.

Recent developments in quantum biology, information theory, and consciousness studies suggest an alternative paradigm: consciousness may be fundamental, with the brain serving as a receiver rather than a generator.

1.2 The Core Hypothesis
We propose the following framework:

A universal informational field exists as a fundamental property of spacetime, analogous to the Higgs field but operating at the level of consciousness-information.

Biological organisms, through DNA, function as fractal antennas capable of coupling with this field.

Electromagnetic processes in the body—particularly coherent biophoton emissions—create a localized conscious field that anchors consciousness to the physical body.

The brain acts as a filter/processor, rendering a limited subset of universal consciousness into individual experience, rather than generating consciousness itself.

Quantum entanglement connects particles in DNA to the source of the universe, enabling non-local information transfer.

1.3 The "Cosmic Wi-Fi" Analogy
Consider the universe as a vast "wireless access point" broadcasting the fundamental information that constitutes consciousness. Our bodies, specifically the DNA within our cells, serve as antennas tuned to receive this broadcast. The brain functions as the processor—interpreting, filtering, and rendering the signal into the individual experience we call "self."

2.2 Quantum Biology: Established Mechanisms
2.2.1 Quantum Coherence in Living Systems
The emerging field of quantum biology provides empirical grounding for how quantum effects function in warm, wet biological environments (May 2026 update, Clinical and Translational Medicine).

Photosynthetic Coherence:
Research at Berkeley Lab (2007) demonstrated long-lived quantum coherence in light-harvesting complexes of green sulfur bacteria, where energy moves through multiple pathways simultaneously via quantum interference. This challenges the assumption that quantum coherence cannot survive in biological systems.

Avian Magnetoreception:
Quantum entanglement between electron spins in cryptochrome molecules enables migratory birds to perceive Earth's magnetic field, functioning as "tiny quantum compasses." This demonstrates that quantum entanglement can persist in biological systems and serve functional roles.

Enzyme Catalysis and Tunneling:
Proton and electron tunneling through energy barriers greatly increases reaction speeds in enzyme catalysis, showing that quantum effects are actively exploited by biological systems.

DNA Proton Tunneling:
Proton tunneling through hydrogen bonds in DNA creates short-lived tautomeric configurations, elevating mispairing likelihood during replication. This mechanism contributes to spontaneous mutations at oncogenic hotspots, demonstrating quantum effects at the genetic level.

2.2.2 Quantum Coherence Protection
Despite decoherence concerns, "biological processes may create protective structures such as hydration shells, dynamic vibrations, or molecular geometries that prevent decoherence and preserve quantum order" (May 2026 review).

Protective Mechanisms:

Hydration shells around DNA maintain quantum coherence

Periodic molecular vibrations (phonons) synchronize quantum states

Frustrated molecular geometries create energy gaps preventing decoherence

Protein scaffolds organize quantum states for functional purposes

2.3 Evidence for Non-Local Consciousness
2.3.1 Veridical Perception in Near-Death Experiences
The strongest empirical challenge to brain-generated consciousness comes from veridical NDEs (vNDEs)—cases where individuals report accurate perceptions of events during cardiac arrest or deep coma when the brain is non-functional.

Key Points:

Patients were clinically unconscious (flat-line EEG, no measurable brain activity)

Perceptions occurred in locations physically inaccessible (outside operating room, behind equipment)

Details were specific and verified by independent sources

Cases challenge the assumption that consciousness requires neural activity

2.3.2 Memory Characteristics of NDEs
A study of 122 NDE survivors using the full 38-item Memory Characteristics Questionnaire found that "memories of near-death experiences are recalled as 'realer' than real events or imagined events" (2022).

Implications:
NDEs cannot be dismissed as hallucinations or confabulations. The phenomenological intensity and consistency across cultures suggest a genuine phenomenon that materialist models cannot adequately explain.

2.3.3 Anomalous Perceptions Beyond NDEs
The literature documents additional phenomena consistent with non-local consciousness:

Shared/ Empathic Death Experiences:
Loved ones partially experience the dying person's death, sometimes at a distance. These experiences cannot be explained by sensory information transfer.

Mindsight:
64% of congenitally blind individuals report visual components during NDEs (Ring & Cooper, 1999). This suggests that NDE perception is not dependent on sensory organs.

Peak in Darien Experiences:
Unwitting encounters with loved ones who died simultaneously or immediately beforehand. Experiencers often have no way of knowing about the death.
