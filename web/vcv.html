The thesis stated precisely

VeriCrypt is the only commercially-ready system that produces a cryptographically-signed, machine-provable, air-gapped compliance artifact (.pqc) that a financial regulator can independently verify without trusting either the bank or the vendor. This is not an incremental improvement over existing PQC assessment tools — it is a categorically different class of evidence.

The regulatory forcing function is explicit and immediate: DORA entered full enforcement on January 17, 2025, covering 20 types of EU financial entities with fines up to 2% of global annual turnover for non-compliance. NIST finalized ML-DSA (FIPS 204), SLH-DSA (FIPS 205), and ML-KEM (FIPS 203) in 2024, triggering what analysts estimate as $4.2 billion in net-new federal and regulated-industry spending in 2025–2026 alone. Every CISO of every EU bank needs a DORA Art. 5–14 compliant cryptographic posture report. VeriCrypt produces one in under 60 seconds. No competitor can make that claim with formal proof.

The ASL Virtual Machine Runtime (Addendum 5, ADR-021) is the architectural invention that makes VeriCrypt unique: regulatory axioms are compiled to deterministic bytecode at build time; at scan time, the VM executes the bytecode against the discovered cryptographic inventory and produces a verifiable schedule trace that any regulator can bit-identically replay. This is the "machine-provable" claim — not marketing language, but a formal computer science property: deterministic execution with bit-identical reproducibility.

The paradigm shift: Every competing PQC tool produces an opinion. VeriCrypt produces a proof. That is the difference between a consulting report and a mathematical theorem, and it is the difference that regulators — and acquirers — pay a premium for.

Why now — the regulatory convergence

DORA is in effect (Jan 17, 2025). 2026 marks the first full year of active supervisory enforcement, including CTPP oversight, on-site inspections, and regulatory examinations. Non-compliance fines: up to 2% of global annual turnover. For a €10B bank, that is €200M per infraction.
NIST PQC standards finalized (2024). The NIST standardization event alone triggered an estimated $4.2B in net-new compliance spending in 2025–2026. VeriCrypt maps directly to DORA Art. 5–14, SEC PQFIF, NCSC Phase 1/2/3, and NIST SP 1800-38.
Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later (HNDL) attacks are active. The Ld > Ha temporal hazard model (formally implemented in VeriCrypt's Quantum Exposure Analyzer) quantifies data already at risk. Regulators and boards are demanding evidence of action, not assurances.

A financial institution can buy a consulting firm's PQC assessment for €500K–€2M. But that assessment is a human opinion. If the regulator challenges it, the bank's only defence is "our consultants said so."
