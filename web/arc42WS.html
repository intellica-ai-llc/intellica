What goes wrong without it

Knowledge lives in heads

Newcomers wait for someone to explain how things fit together.

Decisions get forgotten

Teams re-debate old choices, or break them by accident.

Qualities stay vague

“Fast” and “secure” mean different things to different people.

Machines read it too

AI tools act on what is written and guess what isn’t.

What you will be able to do

Set the goals

Identify stakeholders and write quality goals with numbers instead of adjectives.

Draw the system

Sketch a context view and a Level-1 building-block view, then write one component contract.

Show behaviour

Describe a runtime scenario with a failure branch, and a deployment view.

Record why

Write an architecture decision record (ADR) that lists the road not taken.

Work with AI

Let AI draft a section, then find and fix its mistakes.

Write for agents

Shape documentation so both people and coding agents can use it.

How you will learn

The day is built on how people pick up a new skill: see it done, try it yourself, then check your work with someone else.

See it done

For every section, a finished example from a real-world app, so you know what good looks like before you start.

Do it yourself

Early exercises are mostly filled in. Later ones give you only a template, then a blank page, so the support fades as you grow.

Check with peers

Swap drafts with another team using a short checklist, and retake the opening quiz at the end to see your own progress.

Who it’s for

Developers, tech leads, analysts, testers and managers who build or maintain software and have never written architecture documentation, or never made it stick. No architecture background needed, and no UML.

This is a beginner workshop. If you already write arc42 documents regularly, much of it will be review.

Do I need to know UML?

No. Boxes, arrows and plain words are enough.

Which system do we document?

One your team can explain from memory, such as a school library, a clinic booking system or a maxi-taxi tracker. Please don’t share confidential details.

Will we use AI?

Yes, but you write the first draft yourselves. Then you use AI to find gaps, and you check every line it produces.

What should I bring?

A laptop with a Markdown editor that previews Mermaid diagrams, or a shared document your team can edit.

Is it a certification?

No. It is a practical workshop that ends with a document you can use.

Check before you leave

Rules for AI today

Write a human draft first. Then ask AI why, and what’s missing, not only to write it. You decide: give every AI line a verdict of accepted, rejected or edited, with a reason.

Your 30 days

Week 1: finish sections 1–4 with your real team. Week 2: sections 5–6, one contract per critical block. Week 3: three ADRs, five scenarios, risks. Week 4: try the agent experiment (no docs, a short index plus two contracts, or the full arc42) and share what you find.
