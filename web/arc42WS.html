What arc42 is, and why it matters now

What it is

arc42 is a free, open template for documenting software and system architecture. Its twelve sections give each kind of architecture information a clear place: goals and constraints, context, building blocks, runtime behaviour, deployment, crosscutting concepts, decisions, quality scenarios, risks and a glossary.

It is a structure, not a method, and it doesn’t care about notation: Markdown, text-based diagrams or UML all work. It fits well as docs-as-code, kept in the repository and reviewed like source.

It is free to use, even commercially, with credit to its creators, Gernot Starke and Peter Hruschka.

Why it matters now

Teams lose context. People leave, systems grow and decisions fade. A consistent structure makes the why findable.

AI tools only know what is written. Coding agents generate code quickly but guess at intent. Goals, contracts and recorded decisions are how you give it to them.

Specifying and checking are gaining value. As more code is generated, stating what good looks like and verifying the result become the scarcer skills.

What goes wrong without it

Knowledge lives in heads

Newcomers wait for someone to explain how things fit together.

Decisions get forgotten

Teams re-debate old choices, or break them by accident.

Qualities stay vague

“Fast” and “secure” mean different things to different people.

Machines read it too

AI tools act on what is written and guess what isn’t.

Questions

What is arc42?

A free, open template with twelve sections for writing down how a software system is built and why. It was created by Gernot Starke and Peter Hruschka.

Is this the official arc42 training?

No. This is an independent workshop built on the free arc42 template. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by arc42, and it does not lead to a certification.

I’m an experienced engineer. What’s in it for me?

The template is the easy part. The depth is in measurable quality scenarios, contracts with invariants and error modes, decision records with real alternatives, an AI lab where you red-team generated documentation, and designing docs that coding agents can use without drifting.

I’m new to this. Will I keep up?

Yes. Every section starts with a finished example, early exercises are mostly filled in, and you work in a team. The support fades as you gain confidence.

Will this really cut my AI costs?

It can, if you keep the documentation short and specific. Evidence so far is mixed, and long auto-generated context files can make things worse. The workshop shows you how to test the effect on your own work.

Where and when?

Saturday 5 December 2026, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm at UWI St Augustine. Registered participants get the exact room by email before the day.

Do I need to know UML?

No. Boxes, arrows and plain words are enough.

Which system do we document?

One your team can explain from memory, such as a school library, a clinic booking system or a maxi-taxi tracker. Please don’t share confidential details.

Will we use AI?

Yes, but you write the first draft yourselves. Then you use AI to find gaps, and you check every line it produces.

What should I bring?

A laptop with a Markdown editor that previews Mermaid diagrams, or a shared document your team can edit.

Is it a certification?

No. It is a practical workshop that ends with a document you can use.

Key terms

Stakeholder and concern

A stakeholder is anyone with an interest in the system. A concern is something they care about, such as recovering a lost order.

Quality scenario

A testable statement of stimulus, response and metric, such as “95% of confirmations appear within 3 s at 300 orders per minute”.

Container and component

A container is something you start: an application or data store. A component is a grouping of related functionality behind an interface inside a container.

Contract

What a block promises and expects: pre-conditions, post-conditions, invariants and error modes.

ADR

An architecture decision record: one significant decision with its context, options, decision and consequences.

Idempotency

Repeating a request has the same effect as doing it once.

Rules for AI today

Write a human draft first. Then ask AI why, and what’s missing, not only to write it. You decide: give every AI line a verdict of accepted, rejected or edited, with a reason. Use only your workshop case, and never paste passwords, keys, personal data or client-confidential details.

Check before you leave

Your 30 days

Week 1: finish sections 1–4 with your real team. Week 2: sections 5–6, one contract per critical block. Week 3: three ADRs, five scenarios, risks. Week 4: try the agent experiment (no docs, a short index plus two contracts, or the full arc42) and share what you find.
