Valuation Methodology & Forward-Looking Statements

Each of the four product systems (Cortex, VeriCrypt, VeriChain, Verity) has been independently valued using three-method triangulation: (1) Comparable Company Analysis against publicly-traded and recently-transacted peers in each product's market segment; (2) Precedent Transaction Analysis drawing on disclosed M&A multiples in AI infrastructure, cybersecurity, Layer-1 blockchain, and core banking technology; (3) Discounted Cash Flow projections based on addressable market sizing and conservative penetration assumptions over a 7-year horizon. The Sovereign Thesis

The world is pivoting to sovereign AI. Intellica built it that way from day one.

Most AI companies are retrofitting sovereignty onto cloud-native architectures — bolting on air-gap modes, regional data centres, and compliance wrappers after the fact. That is expensive, brittle, and unconvincing to regulators who can see through it.

Intellica is different: every system was built sovereign-first, on a formally-specified compiler (seedc) and runtime (seedvm) that were designed to operate without external dependencies from the first line of code. There is no cloud mode to disable. Sovereignty is architectural, not operational policy.

McKinsey's March 2026 global survey confirms enterprise demand for sovereign AI has crossed from boardroom aspiration to procurement mandate. IDC forecasts 60% of organisations with digital sovereignty requirements will migrate sensitive workloads by 2028. The window for first-mover positioning closes within 12–24 months.

🏠
Infrastructure sovereignty
Compute, storage, and inference run inside the customer's controlled environment. The system can physically point to where inference is happening and verify it matches regulatory obligations.
🔒
Data sovereignty
Data is processed and retained within jurisdictions that meet legal and regulatory requirements. No metadata exhaust escapes the perimeter. Zero-persistence is architecturally enforced and auditable, not a vendor promise.
🛠
Technological sovereignty
Intellica owns the full stack — compiler, runtime, all five systems. No proprietary API lock-in. No dependency on a foreign model provider who can change terms or go offline. ASL is the IP, not a layer on top of someone else's.
🌍
Operational sovereignty
Policies, audit trails, and controls travel with the workload. If the customer needs to move from cloud to on-prem to air-gap, the system moves with them — no re-engineering, no vendor negotiation.

The Founder

Investors invest in people first.

One founder. Every line of code. Thirty years of enterprise software experience. Two graduate degrees. A career built on a single question that the entire industry is only now learning to ask: why do people actually use — or refuse to use — the technology we build for them?

👨‍💻
Damain Peter Ramsajan
Founder & Sole Author — Intellica AI INC

Damain entered enterprise technology in 2000, in the immediate aftermath of the dot-net crash. The timing was not incidental — it was foundational. His first years in the industry, working at Trinidad Systems Limited and then at CommNett alongside certified experts from Cisco, Microsoft, and Network Appliance, were spent in an environment where customers had just watched billions evaporate on technology that never delivered measurable value. Hype had collapsed. What remained was a discipline that defined the cohort: find real, non-fluffy ways to deliver verifiable solutions to sceptical customers, or don't ship at all. That instinct has never left him.

By 2007 he had formalised that instinct into an MSc in Strategic Business IT at the University of Portsmouth, with a thesis on the acceptability and use of technology — specifically studying the adoption of IBM Maximo's Enterprise Asset Management implementation at BPTT, one of the largest energy operators in the region. His research sat squarely in the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) tradition: the academic framework, originating with Davis (1989), that asks not "does the technology work?" but "will users actually adopt it, and why?" Perceived usefulness. Perceived ease of use. The gap between deployment and behaviour change. These were not peripheral questions for Damain — they were his intellectual foundation, applied to enterprise software at a major oil and gas operator, producing findings that practitioners in governance training and IT implementation could directly act on.

That foundation shaped every engagement that followed. Working across energy sector, government, and enterprise clients on IT governance, architecture, and training initiatives, his roles always contained an architecture dimension — but always through the lens of the human adoption problem. A technically correct system that users don't adopt is not a delivered system. It is a liability with a user interface. He never forgot that.

In 2019, approaching 50, he travelled to Aston University in Birmingham to study a Postgraduate Diploma in Software Engineering, with a specific focus on engineering multi-agent systems. He has been building complex systems in Python, TypeScript, and Rust since — each one more architecturally rigorous than the last. The academic grounding in formal methods combined with thirty years of watching real users interact with real enterprise software produced something rare: a systems architect who simultaneously understands what formal correctness requires and what actual adoption demands. Those two things are usually held by different people. In Damain they are the same person.

The result is the Intellica portfolio. Every design decision across ASL, Cortex, VeriCrypt, VeriChain, and Verity reflects both constraints simultaneously. VeriCrypt's one-command CISO deployment model is a TAM decision — reduce perceived effort to zero so the adoption barrier disappears entirely. Cortex's natural language interface over SAP, Maximo, Salesforce, and Snowflake is a TAM decision — replace the interfaces users hate with the interface they already know. Verity's seven-day migration with cryptographic proof is a TAM decision — make the switching cost so low and the evidence so clear that no procurement committee can manufacture a reason to delay. Every system is formally verified. Every system is designed to be used.

In 2026, 42% of enterprise AI initiatives have been abandoned — more than double the prior year. Not because the models don't work. Because deployment and adoption are not the same thing, and most AI builders have never studied the difference. Damain studied it in 2007. He has been building for it ever since.
