The thesis stated precisely

Verity is the fourth product on the Intellecta platform, and by far the most ambitious in market scope. Every bank in the world runs on a core banking system. Most of those systems are 20–40 years old, batch-processing, cloud-dependent, unable to handle AI agents as first-class entities, and incapable of producing a mathematically-provable audit trail for regulators. Verity can do all three — simultaneously, in the same binary. And it does it while running ASL-compiled banking products that cannot compile if they violate Reg DD, Reg Z, or Reg E. While providing a Merkle-proofed, event-sourced, append-only ledger that makes migration validation instantaneous rather than batch-reconciled. While treating AI agents as first-class banking customers with their own accounts (1A1A), their own on-chain identity on VeriChain, and capability-governed spending limits that make "Excessive Agency" structurally impossible.

It is the most comprehensive application of that foundation, bringing all four systems' capabilities to bear on the largest, most regulated, and most transformation-hungry market in the world: banking.

Why €480M — and why it's the right number at this stage

This range is deliberately calibrated against two anchors. The floor (€380M) reflects the deep-tech IP value of the architecture — the TLA+ ledger, the Lean 4 compliance engine, the multi-TEE concurrent operation, the ASL product compiler, and the VTVP theorem validation pipeline — on top of the already-proven founder-owned platform. This is substantially higher than VeriCrypt's €480M base case for one reason: Verity subsumes VeriCrypt's use case (PQC compliance) within the banking vertical, adds the entire core banking product layer on top, and enters a market that is 8× larger by TAM than the PQC compliance niche.

The ceiling (€620M) reflects the Thought Machine comparable adjusted for Verity's architectural advantages. Thought Machine reached $2.7B at roughly £35–47M ARR — approximately 60–75× ARR. At Verity's Phase 1 pricing (€45K–€450K per bank annually per the v21 licensing model) with 5–15 early customers, the revenue multiple analogy produces a comparable range. The key question is not "is Verity worth more than Thought Machine architecturally?" (it clearly is) but "does Verity have Thought Machine's distribution?" (it does not yet). The range reflects that honest asymmetry.

The Thought Machine precedent is the most important data point in this valuation. Thought Machine has no formal verification. No TLA+ ledger. No multi-TEE operation. No agent-native architecture. No sovereign deployment. No 7-day migration claim with cryptographic proof. It reached $2.7B. Verity has all of those things, plus the same proven founder-owned platform that already runs three other production systems. The valuation gap between Verity and Thought Machine is distribution — and distribution is a solvable problem, not an architectural one.
